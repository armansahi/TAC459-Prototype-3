Founder Intake

A five-minute chat that turns a founder's messy hiring notes into a clear role brief and a scorecard recruiters can actually use.

The problem

Contrario's recruiters are only as good as the brief they get from the founder.

What founders send today

"We need a strong full-stack engineer, ideally from healthtech." Vague, missing pay, nothing on what to avoid, and no way to tell a good candidate from a great one.

What that costs

Recruiters guess, submit the wrong people, and keep pinging the founder with questions. Prototype 1's copilot answers questions from the brief, so a thin brief means thin answers.

How it works

The founder talks. The AI interviews. A brief builds itself on the side.

The founder describes the roleMessy notes are fine. No form to fill in.
The AI asks one follow-up at a timeWhen an answer is vague ("strong engineer"), it pushes for something concrete: a company they'd poach from, a project the person would ship.
The brief fills in liveMust-haves, dealbreakers, pay, and why a candidate would say yes. Anything confidential goes to a private section. Anything not said is flagged as a question, never made up.
It ends with a 100-point scorecard4 to 5 criteria with points and a plain description of what earns full marks. The founder exports the brief for recruiters, with internal notes stripped out.

What it looks like

An illustrative example, written by hand to show the format. It is not real model output.

The conversation

We're Lumen, a seed-stage startup. Need a founding senior full-stack engineer in SF, someone strong from healthtech or dev tools.
Got it. "Strong" can mean a lot of things. Which company would you most want to hire this person from, and why?
Someone from Redox or Healthie. They've already dealt with messy EHR integrations.
Helpful. Is there anything that would rule a candidate out, even with the right resume?
No pure front-end people. And keep the $190k top of range between us.
